# M2 Steel Core — narration script

Target ~2:40. Each block is one TTS render; filenames match `vo/NN_name.mp3`.

---

## 01_hook  (over: hero turntable)
This is a one-to-one Browning M2, and almost all of it is 3D printed. But the part
doing the work isn't printed at all. It's a single piece of steel tube — and that one
change turns a display piece into something you can actually pick up by the barrel.

## 02_problem  (over: hero turntable continuing, then printed-only)
A printed receiver this size has a problem. It flexes. The model this is based on
solves it by running a four-foot wooden dowel down the middle and gluing everything to
it. That works, but the plastic is still carrying the load — every mount, every hinge,
every time you set it down.

## 03_idea  (over: crossfade full → steel only)
So here's the swap. One length of two-by-three rectangular steel tube, an eighth of an
inch wall, twenty-three inches long. Everything structural bolts or welds to that. The
printed parts stop being the gun and become skins over it.

## 04_steel  (over: steel-only turntable)
This is everything that's metal. The core tube. A quarter-inch plate welded across the
front. A DOM socket for the barrel. Half-inch tabs for the pintle. Eighth-inch tabs
inside the tube for the cover hinge, so hinge load never touches plastic. Eleven pounds
of steel, all of it standard imperial stock you can buy in twelve-inch lengths.

## 05_section  (over: cross-section sweep)
In section you can see how it works. The steel box runs the full length. The engine
cradle drops in through the top opening. The printed panels sit four tenths of a
millimetre off the steel and screw to it — thirty-six M3 thread-forming screws straight
into the tube wall, no tapping, no rivnut tool.

## 06_charging  (over: charging handle detail + travel)
The charging handle works. Three printed parts sandwich the receiver wall and ride in
the slot that's already there — no extra metal removed for it. A hundred and fifty
millimetres of travel, spring returned.

## 07_trigger  (over: trigger mechanism detail)
The trigger works too. One lever pivots on a pin through both tube walls, runs forward
through a slot in the backplate, and lifts a microswitch wired to the engine. Five
degrees of travel fires it. And the backplate bolts on rather than welding shut, so you
can still get at all of it.

## 08_barrel  (over: barrel jacket + EMT)
The barrel is one-inch EMT conduit — the electrical stuff, about twelve dollars for ten
feet. It slips into the socket with six tenths of a millimetre of clearance and a
hundred millimetres of engagement. Two set screws tapped into the socket hold it, and
the jacket has access holes underneath so a hex key reaches them. The barrel comes off
without taking anything else apart.

## 09_jacket  (over: jacket rotating)
The perforated jacket is the one part of the barrel worth printing. It's the M2's
signature, and it slides straight over the conduit.

## 10_outro  (over: hero turntable, then title card)
Every file is on GitHub — the STEP for the tube, the STLs, a one-to-one wrap template
you can print and tape around the steel to mark every cut and hole, and the full build
notes. Links below.

The skin geometry is derived from HappyBattleSheep's M2 on Thingiverse, used under
Creative Commons Attribution. The steel design and everything mechanical is released
Creative Commons non-commercial share-alike.

It's an airsoft replica. Check your local law on replica markings before you build one.
